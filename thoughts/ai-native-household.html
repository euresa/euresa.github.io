Towards an AI-Native Household

October 2026

As anyone who has been in a long-term relationship knows, it takes a lot of communication, planning, and logistics to keep a household and our lives running smoothly. At work these days, the idea of starting new projects or maintaining existing work without the use of Claude/agents seems ridiculous given how helpful this technology is. I'm hoping to bring this kind of uplift back home to help my wife and me maintain our lives and achieve our goals.

I think this is a pretty obvious move. In our household we (and probably most households around our age group) have already been leveraging tech pretty heavily. We keep track of a lot of our records in Google Docs/Sheets, organize information in Notion, and sometimes plan goals or household tasks in Trello (my wife is the CEO of our lives, and she has a passion for organizing information). We also really enjoy talking to Amazon's Alexa, and we have multiple such devices in our apartments. There's also a lot of Gmailing each other, which we probably do more than text since we're with each other so often, and when we need to send a written message it is usually for sharing a resource or making a note of an idea.

We've built up a pretty decent corpus of messages, emails, and documents related to our household, lives, and interests. This is all information that agents can use and analyze to help us in the future.

I've recently been learning about Andrej Karpathy's LLM wiki pattern and think it could be a good way to help boil down this household-related information into a maintainable and self-updating system. I'm not a super huge Obsidian fan, but it has been helpful for keeping an eye on how the information gets organized and for spot-checking the raw files and ingestion flow. I used Claude Code on my local MacBook Air to help create this initial vault and ingest the raw sources, and I back up an encrypted version of the vault in my personal GitHub.

There are a lot of great harnesses, many of them open source, that can be used as personal AI assistant agents for things like household management. I considered Claude Code, pi.dev, OpenClaw, OpenCode, and Hermes Agent, and ultimately chose Hermes after some experimentation since it seems really tailored for what we're trying to accomplish. It can be used with OpenRouter for model usage, which has a lot of cheap (or free! shoutout to NVIDIA Nemotron Ultra) model options. It also has a really nice Discord bot integration, and my wife is a big Discord fan.

I originally developed this on my MacBook Air and needed my computer to be on for the Discord gateway server to be running. After an expected amount of hammering out permissions and integrations with Notion, Google's API, Discord, and the LLM wiki's design and maintenance cycle, it started working quite well.

I've promoted it to a $12/month, 1 CPU, 2GB memory VPS on DigitalOcean so it can run 24/7, and I have my Claude Code session on my MacBook Air SSH into it for management. I could totally get a small Raspberry Pi to keep on 24/7 in my apartment and run this on, but I really don't want to make that kind of upfront investment right now given how quickly things continue to change in the AI world. For similar reasons, I'd rather not buy something like a Mac mini and run a smaller open weight LLM to make token usage free: I can already just use NVIDIA's free models, and the upfront cost of a new computer doesn't make sense to me if I only need to pay about $12/month in hosting and maybe $10/month in compute.

Lessons Learned

  • I originally tried to create the LLM wiki vault with Hermes powered by OpenRouter models, but I often got greedy and kept creeping up which model and effort levels I was using until I ended up spending a lot more money than I would have had I just used something like a Claude Code Pro (or Max) one-month subscription for the work. This is what motivated me to build the "Tell Claude" flow into the Hermes agent, for when deeper research or automation flows need to be set up on the VPS agent via Discord communication.
  • I should have spent more time learning about different ways to design the LLM wiki's structure and integration flow. There are a lot of decisions to be made around how new information is inserted into the vault, what kind of information deserves to be in our household vault, and what information can be left out with just links to more detailed information. I think vault management and design will continue to need tweaking as I learn what is working well and what is not when it comes to integrating household-specific information into conversations.
